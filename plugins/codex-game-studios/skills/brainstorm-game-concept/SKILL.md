# Brainstorm Game Concept

Explore meaningfully different possibilities before converging on a concept whose riskiest assumptions can be tested.

## Frame the opportunity

Gather only context that affects the concept: intended player, platform and session shape, team and schedule, business or creative constraints, inspirations, prohibited directions, and desired emotional experience. Inspect existing notes before asking questions. When information is missing but not decisive, state a reversible assumption and continue.

Express the opportunity as a design tension or player promise rather than a list of features. Separate fixed constraints from preferences and unexplored choices.

## Generate distinct directions

Create three to five directions that differ in their core verb, decision pattern, progression, or social relationship—not merely setting or art style. For each direction, define:

- a one-sentence player promise;
- player role and fantasy;
- target player and play context;
- core loop with action, feedback, consequence, and renewed choice;
- two to four design pillars that constrain later decisions;
- sources of tension, mastery, expression, or surprise;
- minimum viable experience and explicit non-goals;
- strongest differentiator, largest feasibility risk, and largest fun risk;
- the cheapest prototype or research test that could invalidate the premise.

Do not hide tradeoffs behind a blended option. If two directions are compatible, first evaluate them independently and explain what is lost by combining them.

## Compare and converge

Compare directions against the stated player promise, distinctiveness, scope, technical uncertainty, content burden, learnability, replayability, and team fit. Use qualitative judgments unless the user supplied reliable quantitative criteria. Recommend one direction, explain why it wins, and name the evidence that could reverse the recommendation.

Turn the selected direction into a concept brief using [the game concept template](../../assets/templates/game-concept.md). Include:

- high concept and player fantasy;
- audience, platform, session, and control assumptions;
- pillars and anti-pillars;
- moment-to-moment and longer-term loops;
- verbs, goals, obstacles, failure, recovery, and progression;
- representative play scenario;
- visual, audio, narrative, and interaction identity at concept level;
- scope boundary and first playable slice;
- hypotheses, risks, open questions, and validation plan.

Apply [design quality guidance](../../references/design-quality.md) to keep pillars actionable, loops causal, and claims testable.

## Hand off at the right boundary

Use `$design-game` when the concept is stable enough to map whole-game systems. Use `$design-game-feature` when one feature needs a bounded specification. If the user asks for a playable experiment, hand the selected hypothesis and success criteria to an available prototype or implementation workflow; do not turn the concept brief into hidden technical architecture.

Finish with the recommended concept, rejected alternatives and their tradeoffs, assumptions, validation tests, and the next decision. Mark any user choice still required instead of presenting it as settled.
